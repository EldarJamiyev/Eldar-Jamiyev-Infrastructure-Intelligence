# LinkedIn Hook

Write the opening 1-2 lines of a LinkedIn post — the part visible before the "see more" cutoff (~210 characters on mobile). This is the only job of a hook: stop the scroll and earn the click. Everything else is a different task.

## Before writing

Ask (briefly, inline, not as a formal interview) if not already clear from context:
- What's the actual news/insight/lesson? (the real content, not the framing)
- Who is this for — technical peers, hiring managers, general audience?
- Any numbers, before/after, or concrete outcome to lead with?

## Hook patterns (pick what fits the content, don't force one)

- **Contrarian/reversal**: state the common belief, then break it. "Everyone says X. We did the opposite."
- **Specific number**: a stat or outcome that's oddly precise (precision reads as true). "37% faster. Here's what changed."
- **Confession/vulnerability**: admit a mistake or hard moment before the lesson. "I almost shipped a bug that would've cost us the client."
- **Direct address**: name the reader's exact situation. "If you're building a chatbot with Ollama, read this first."
- **Before/after**: one line each, stark contrast.

## Rules

- No throat-clearing ("I'm excited to share...", "Big news..."). Start mid-thought.
- One idea per hook. Not a summary of the whole post.
- Concrete over abstract: "cut response time from 4s to 400ms" beats "improved performance."
- Short sentences. Fragments are fine. This is not formal writing.
- Never open with a question unless the answer is genuinely surprising.

## Output

Give 3 hook variants (different pattern each), then let the user pick before writing the rest of the post. Don't write the full post unless asked.
